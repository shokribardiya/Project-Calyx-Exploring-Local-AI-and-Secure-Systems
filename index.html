Local intelligence. Defensive engineering. Systems designed with control in mind.

CALYX is an Oblivion initiative that studies how AI can support defensive security work, and how local models can become more practical to use. It is not a trained foundation model, and it does not perform autonomous cyber defense.

Local models offer private, controllable workflows. Using several of them together creates orchestration problems. Security-related AI needs more than a capable model: it needs boundaries, verification and behavior a person can understand.

This site separates three states of work: implemented experimental direction Each claim carries one of them.

Question: can memory be trusted by the model?

Nythos compiles a workspace packet that marks model-generated candidates as UNVERIFIED and treats stored entries as data, not instructions. A recorded test also found a limit: a claim contradicting a stored constraint (“may use requests” vs. a standard-library-only rule) was not flagged by the lexical conflict engine. We publish that limit.

Question: does a helper model improve a primary model?

Angra estimates suggest small gains in text and coding with a cost in efficiency. These are heuristic and unverified; a methodology for real measurement is the next step.

Question: what hardware does a coordinated workflow need?

Hardware-conscious operation is a design objective. No hardware requirements have been published.

Part of a wider body of work.

Oblivion is an independent systems and research organization. CALYX inherits its habit of treating science and art as neighbors: star charts beside marbled paper, circuitry beside eyes that watch.

Security is an engineering discipline.

Local execution can give a user more control, but it does not by itself guarantee security or privacy. Outcomes depend on implementation, configuration, dependencies and maintenance. CALYX has not been externally audited; OWASP ASVS and NIST SSDF are used as engineering references, not certifications.
